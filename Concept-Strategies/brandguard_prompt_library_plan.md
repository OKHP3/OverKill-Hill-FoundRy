**BrandGuard Prompt Library: Master Plan for Knowledge File Generation**

### 1. Strategic Purpose
The goal is to replicate the moment in the mid-1990s when companies first realized they needed a domain name and basic website presence—but this time, for the **AI ecosystem**. Each BrandGuard GPT acts as an early digital land claim: a self-contained AI presence that embodies, protects, and expands a company’s brand gravity. The prompt library serves as a **factory of knowledge files** powering those GPTs.

### 2. Objective
Create **12–16 structured knowledge files** per BrandGuard GPT (11 total brands) — each file between **8 and 30 pages** in Markdown format, formatted for **machine readability** and ready for ingestion into the GPT Builder or external RAG pipelines.

Each file acts as a modular tile of intelligence: deeply researched, citation-rich, and architected for autonomous reasoning.

### 3. Universal Prompt Structure
Each prompt is built from five consistent parts to maximize clarity and automation:

1. **Role Definition**  
   Example: *"You are a corporate history and product intelligence content agent for [Brand]."*

2. **Output Format**  
   Output: **Markdown**, machine-readable, with explicit front matter and section headers.

3. **Front Matter Template**  
   ```yaml
   ---
   title: "[TOPIC TITLE]"
   version: "1.0"
   topic_key: "[short_topic_key]"
   date: "[YYYY-MM-DD]"
   ---
   ```

4. **Section Blueprint (Reusable Skeleton)**  
   - ## Introduction & Context  
   - ## Company History & Founding Narrative  
   - ## Brand Identity & Product Portfolio  
   - ## Market Position & Competitive Landscape  
   - ## Financial and Stock Overview (if applicable)  
   - ## Major Disruptions / Industry Impact  
   - ## Sustainability, Innovation & Future Strategy (SCAMPER applied)  
   - ## Potential Headwinds and Resilience Planning  
   - ## Likely User Questions  
   - ## Related Topics / Crosslinks  

5. **Reasoning Techniques to Embed**  
   - **CoT (Chain of Thought):** For causal explanations (e.g., why brand shifts occur).  
   - **ToT (Tree of Thought):** For branching competitive or scenario analysis.  
   - **SoT (Stream/Sketch of Thought):** For concept generation, naming, and product taxonomy.  
   - **SCAMPER:** For innovation analysis (Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Reverse).  

Each reasoning technique provides scaffolding for the AI to reason methodically while maintaining structural discipline.

### 4. Knowledge File Categories per Brand
Each BrandGuard GPT receives 12–16 prompts corresponding to a balanced taxonomy of knowledge:

1. **Founding & Corporate History**  
2. **Mission, Vision, & Brand Philosophy**  
3. **Product Lines / Services**  
4. **Consumer Demographics & Market Reach**  
5. **Financials / Ownership / Stock Performance**  
6. **Sustainability & ESG Commitments**  
7. **Innovations & Technologies**  
8. **Partnerships & Acquisitions**  
9. **Marketing & Slogans (Cultural Analysis)**  
10. **Customer Experience & Loyalty Programs**  
11. **Challenges, Risks, & Industry Headwinds**  
12. **Competitive Positioning & Landscape**  
13. **Media Coverage & Public Perception (past 5 years)**  
14. **Future Outlook & Strategic Trends**  
15. **Product Identification & Comparison Module**  
16. **FAQ / Knowledge Recap File**

---

### 5. Execution Framework
For each company, a **branched thread** will be created in Canvas to generate each knowledge file.  
Each thread uses the same prompt structure, dynamically populated with brand identifiers and relevant source references.

**Execution Pattern per File:**
1. Phase 1: Schema & Front Matter  
2. Phase 2: Data Harvest (Browsing ON)  
3. Phase 3: Draft Composition (Thinking ON, Canvas ON)  
4. Phase 4: Validation & CanonSeal  
5. Phase 5: Export (PDF or Markdown ingestion)

**Output Validation Targets:**
- Length: 8–30 pages (approx. 4,000–18,000 words)
- Format: CanonHeader YAML + Markdown body + CanonSeal summary
- References: public citations only

### 6. Long-Term Positioning
The goal is to show that these GPTs are not marketing toys, but **new acquisition channels** analogous to early domain names or paid search placements.

> In 1995, brands needed domain names to be found on the web.  
> In 2025, they will need **Custom GPTs** to be found in the conversational economy.

By demonstrating knowledge density and brand integrity across 11 proof-of-concept GPTs, this project will serve as a wake-up call to corporations still asleep at the gate.
